The click

The lid rides a spring-loaded cam. Pushed past halfway it snaps the rest of the way — open or shut, never resting in between — and that snap is the famous clink.

Here: drag the lid and it follows your finger around the hinge — arc it open or shut the way a thumb would. Double-tap to open and light in one go, or pinch lid-top and base-bottom and squeeze until it pops.

Striking

A knurled wheel scrapes a flint pressed against it from below by a spring. One firm downward roll of the thumb shaves off white-hot sparks and throws them into the wick.

Here: flick down on the wheel — lazy rolls just grind without sparking, and the wheel is only exposed while the lid is open.

A windproof flame

The chimney feeds the flame through its rows of holes while breaking up gusts, so wind bends the flame instead of blowing it out. To kill it you starve it of air: cap the chimney, or close the lid.

Here: a fast swipe through the flame only makes it flicker; a slow pass — or a finger held over the chimney — smothers it. Closing the lid always puts it out.

The phone counts as weather too, once you allow motion: a steady shake snuffs a lit flame, a sharp jolt slams it sideways (or wakes a hiding ember), and tilting steers which way is up — hold it over and the flame licks up the case.

Fuel

The case holds an insert packed with cotton. Fluid soaks the cotton and climbs the wick by capillary action, so the flame burns vapor at the tip, never liquid. As the tank runs down the flame shrinks and sputters; bone-dry cotton leaves sparks with nothing to catch. Real ones also lose fuel to evaporation between uses.

Maintenance: pull the insert out of the case, fold back the felt pad on its underside, and soak the cotton — stop when it feels full, never flood it.

Here, the quick way: close the lid and hold a finger on the bottom of the case; it glugs itself full. Or do it properly: with the flame out and the lid open, pull the insert up out of the case, peel back the felt pad, and hold a finger on the cotton while the can pours.

Flint

Every strike wears the flint shorter, and a worn flint sparks weakly or not at all.

Maintenance: unscrew the brass spring screw in the base of the insert, tip out the old stub, drop a fresh flint into the tube, and screw the spring back down.

Here: the quick refill hold seats a new flint — or pull the insert out and do it by hand: circle a finger on the brass screw to back it out, tap the tube to tip out the stub, drop the fresh flint in, and screw the spring back down. Weak dud showers mean it’s time.

The wick

The wick is cotton braided around copper wire, so it stands upright in the chimney. The tip slowly chars with use.

Maintenance: tug the wick up with pliers to expose fresh braid, snip it level with the chimney, and it’s good for years.

Here: with the flame out, a blue ball appears on a thread above the wick. Drag it left or right and the wick folds that way, like a joystick — it sits clear of the lighter so your finger never covers what you’re adjusting. Drag back the other way to pull the wick straight. The ball only appears when bending is possible: it won’t budge while anything is still burning.

Bend it low and swipe the flame away to hide it as an ember (tap the lighter, scoop upward, or snap the phone to call it back).
